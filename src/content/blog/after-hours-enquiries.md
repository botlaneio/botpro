Most small businesses close in the evening. Their customers don't. People look for a dentist, a caterer or a machining supplier when they finally have a quiet moment, which is often after dinner. So some of your enquiries will always arrive when nobody is there to answer.

What happens next is up to you. There are three ways to handle it.

## 1. Reply the next morning

This is what most businesses do, and it can work. It costs nothing to set up.

The risk is the gap. Someone with a question at 10pm often asks more than one business. By the time you reply at 10am, they may already have an answer from someone else.

If you go this way, make the next-morning reply a habit, not a hope: check enquiries first thing, before anything else, and answer every one the same day.

## 2. Send an instant reply

An instant reply goes out the moment an enquiry arrives, in your name. It doesn't answer the question, but it tells the person three things: you've received their message, a real person will reply, and when.

That's often enough to stop them looking elsewhere. Here's a simple one you can adapt:

> Thanks for getting in touch with [Business]. We've received your message and we'll reply by [time] tomorrow. If it's urgent, call or WhatsApp us on [number] during opening hours, [days and times].

A few rules make it work:

- **Promise a time you'll keep.** "By 11am tomorrow" beats "as soon as possible".
- **Keep it short.** It's a receipt, not a brochure.
- **Send it in your name**, from an address people can reply to.

Pair it with a single place where every enquiry lands, such as a shared sheet, so nothing gets lost between your email, phone and WhatsApp.

## 3. Let a chatbot answer the common questions

Many night-time questions are the same few: Are you open on Sunday? Do you deliver to my area? Roughly how much does it cost? What do I need to bring?

A chatbot trained on your own services, prices and hours can answer those at any time, and pass anything else to you. Two things matter:

- **It only knows what you give it.** Write down your real answers first; that list is most of the work.
- **It should hand over, not guess.** When it isn't sure, it should take the person's details and tell them you'll be in touch.

## Which one is right for you?

Start with the cheapest thing that closes the gap. If most of your enquiries are simple questions, a chatbot saves you the most time. If they need a conversation, an instant reply and a reliable next-morning routine are usually enough.

Whatever you choose, test it: send yourself an enquiry at night and see what happens.
